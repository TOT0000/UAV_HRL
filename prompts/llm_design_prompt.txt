Design additional state features and fixed reward weights for the UAV post-disaster data collection task below. These features will augment the observations of a centralized TD3 movement policy.

Return one JSON candidate containing feature definitions, reward weights, and executable Python code.

1. Task and control scope

A fleet of 16 UAVs operates in a standard 1000 x 1000 m area. Each episode lasts 60 seconds and contains 2 to 8 regions of interest (RoIs).

UAVs discover RoIs, acquire visual sensing (VS) data and communication (COM) data from ground sources (SRs), and deliver the data through a UAV relay network to a ground station (GS).

VS data generation requires valid sensing geometry. Its useful amount depends on RoI coverage and image quality at capture time. COM data is uploaded from an SR to its serving UAV. Both types may wait in queues and traverse multiple relay hops. Useful data is credited when it reaches the GS within its deadline.

VS and COM deadlines are 2.5 seconds and 2.0 seconds, respectively, measured from data generation and including source-side waiting and subsequent transmissions.

The objectives are to improve energy efficiency and reduce end-to-end deadline violations:

Energy efficiency =
total useful data delivered to the GS within its deadlines
/
total movement energy of all UAVs.

This ratio uses totals over the entire episode. Interpret energy consumption, backlog, and deadline violations together with task service: lower values can also result from acquiring or delivering less data.

An existing task allocator determines UAV tasks and service targets, and a routing controller selects packet next hops. TD3 controls movement for the VS/COM UAVs identified by movement_mask. Search UAVs follow the existing search controller, and hovering UAVs remain hovering.

TD3 acts once per second. Each movement interval contains four communication slots of 0.25 seconds. Each UAV has three action components: speed, horizontal heading, and vertical speed, giving 48 joint action dimensions.

A UAV may serve VS and COM tasks simultaneously with different targets. Movement affects sensing quality, SR uploads, backhaul connectivity and capacity, queue service, and energy consumption. Design features that help the policy recognize these relationships and their trade-offs.

Assign a fixed reward_weight w_k to each feature:

sum_k abs(w_k) <= 1

Positive weights reward larger feature values; negative weights penalize them. A zero weight retains the feature in the state without directly contributing to reward.

The host program computes:

s_aug = concat(s_original, f)
r_extra = sum_k w_k * f_k
r_total = r_base + beta * r_extra

Your function computes only f. The original state is preserved, and the original reward remains:

r_base = B_timely_Mbit - lambda * E_movement_J - P_C9 - P_C10 - P_COM

B_timely_Mbit is useful data delivered to the GS within its deadlines during the movement interval, measured in Mbit.
E_movement_J is the movement energy of all UAVs during that interval, measured in J.
P_C9, P_C10, and P_COM are the existing sensing-geometry and communication-distance penalties defined in the interface.

Features are computed before the action; the original reward reflects outcomes during the subsequent movement interval. The training procedure manages lambda.
