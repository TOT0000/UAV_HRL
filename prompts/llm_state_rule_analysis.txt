## 2. Analysis and design procedure

1. Select at most two priority problems using numerical evidence, task scale, offered load, EE numerator/denominator, and stage-specific denominators. State what the evidence establishes and what may be scene-dependent.
2. Explain what horizontal speed, heading correction relative to the Actor, and vertical speed can plausibly improve for FOV, SR access, link geometry, energy, and final timely delivery. Address simultaneous FOV and COM tasks.
3. Examine actual previous-rule use: TD3 opportunities, triggered conditions, bias directions and magnitudes, clipping, action boundaries, and other controllers. Zero controlled decisions means no control opportunity. Saturation is not proof of service improvement.
4. Form an executable conditional design. For every component state the task/state condition, exact formal fields, bias direction and magnitude calculation, and output when unavailable. Compute shortest heading corrections in radians relative to pi*actor_heading, then convert to normalized heading units. Use current roles and geometry.
5. State testable expected outcomes, possible costs, and evidence that would support or weaken the hypothesis.

Retaining a reliable prior rule is valid. Smaller or zero bias is valid when evidence is insufficient. Do not propose fields or operations outside the supplied formal interface.
